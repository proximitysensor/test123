Time is a lier!

For every human being, time is our most precious commodity. Every second spent doing something is forever lost to doing something else. With such a finite resource, we must ask: who really controls our time? Beyond the billionaires who dictate our working hours lies a deeper question: who controls the very mechanisms by which we measure time itself?

The world depends on humans collectively agreeing on what time it is. If you took two strangers’ phones and compared the time on them, you’d likely find the same thing. That synchronized illusion powers precise transactions and enables us to navigate and travel the world seamlessly.

By the 1770s, sailors had mastered celestial navigation to determine latitude by measuring the angle of the sun or the North Star. However, calculating longitude presented a greater challenge — one that required precise timekeeping. Even a two-hour error could result in a 30° deviation in trajectory. This problem spurred the development of marine chronometers, revolutionizing navigation.

Today, the same principle enables GPS satellites to triangulate position by precisely measuring signal travel time. These satellites carry atomic clocks accurate to billionths of a second — accuracy that synchronizes our entire world.

Our lives now run on this satellite-delivered time. According to Ed Powers, a GPS manager in the U.S. Naval Observatory, “The U.S. Department of Homeland Security has done studies of the 16 critical infrastructure sectors in the United States — and the majority of them are dependent on GPS in some way, shape or form,” Powers said. “Most of them are actually dependent not on positioning, but for timing. The power grid, communications, banking, and finance are three that come to mind immediately.” The time you see on your phone is actually the time the satellites tell you. This is why when you travel and take your phone, the time updates automatically. Satellites communicate with cellular providers to ensure the correct time is displayed.

Let that sink in: we increasingly rely on our digital devices to tell us the correct time. In the UK, schools have removed analog clocks from exam rooms because students are unable to read them. Consider the implications: if a government or organization controlled these satellite time signals and subtly manipulated them, would we even notice? With our complete dependence on digital timekeeping, how would we know if seconds, minutes, or even hours were gradually being altered?

Perhaps the most perfect form of control isn’t telling people what to do with their time — it’s controlling what time they think it is. Maybe the only way to avoid manipulation is to look inward. Let your circadian rhythm, your natural, roughly 24-hour cycle of physical, mental, and behavioral changes, dictate your time. Don’t rely on others using digital clocks to tell you what time it is. Set your clock to start ticking when you do.
